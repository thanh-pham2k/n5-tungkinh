# Lesson 36 — Urbanisation

### Reading Passage

The Growth of Urban Areas

A. Urbanisation describes the increasing concentration of people in towns and cities. This process often occurs when people move from rural areas in search of employment, education, and public services. Growing cities can create economic opportunities because businesses, workers, and customers are located close together. However, rapid growth may also place pressure on existing infrastructure.

B. Housing is one of the most visible challenges. When the number of residents increases faster than new homes can be built, accommodation may become more expensive. Lower-income households can be pushed towards distant neighbourhoods where transport and services are limited. Building additional housing can help, but planners must also consider its location and connection to workplaces.

C. Urban growth can also affect the environment. Expanding roads and buildings may replace natural areas, while traffic and energy use can contribute to pollution. Some city governments respond by protecting green spaces and encouraging public transportation. These measures aim to reduce environmental damage without preventing economic activity.

D. Supporters of compact urban development argue that placing homes, shops, and services closer together can reduce the need for long journeys. Critics worry that high-density neighbourhoods may become uncomfortable if public facilities do not expand with the population. Both perspectives suggest that density alone does not determine whether a city is pleasant to live in.

E. Urbanisation is neither automatically beneficial nor inevitably harmful. Its outcomes depend on planning decisions, public investment, and how fairly opportunities are distributed. Cities that coordinate housing, transport, and environmental protection may be better prepared to accommodate future residents.

### Paraphrasing

- increasing concentration of people → growing share of residents in urban areas
- pressure on infrastructure → greater demand on public systems
- pushed towards distant neighbourhoods → forced to seek housing farther away
- reduce the need for long journeys → make lengthy travel less necessary
- accommodate future residents → provide for a growing population

### Common Mistakes

- Main Idea vs Detail: Housing prices là chi tiết quan trọng, nhưng không phải chủ đề bao quát toàn bài.
- Matching Headings: Đoạn D không chỉ nói ưu điểm của density mà so sánh cả lợi ích và rủi ro.
- Inference: Xây thêm nhà chưa đủ nếu thiếu transport và services.
