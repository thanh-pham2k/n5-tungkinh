## Passage A — The Fifteen-Minute Neighbourhood

A. Some urban planners support neighbourhoods where residents can reach essential services within a short walk or cycle ride. The aim is not to prevent people from travelling farther, but to make daily activities less dependent on long journeys. Nearby shops, schools, and public spaces may save time and encourage local interaction.

B. However, creating such neighbourhoods is not simply a matter of placing more buildings close together. Services must be affordable and suitable for different residents. A nearby supermarket may offer little practical benefit to households that cannot afford its products. Safe streets and accessible routes are also necessary for people with different mobility needs.

C. Critics question whether the same model can work equally well in every area. Existing neighbourhoods differ in population density, land use, and available resources. Supporters respond that the idea can guide gradual improvements rather than require identical designs everywhere. The most useful principle is improving access to everyday needs while respecting local conditions.

## Passage B — Community Spaces in Modern Cities

A. Public libraries, parks, and community centres provide places where residents can spend time without necessarily making purchases. Supporters argue that such spaces encourage interaction among people from different backgrounds. They may also offer opportunities for learning, exercise, and community activities.

B. Yet the presence of a public space does not guarantee that everyone will use it. A park may be difficult to reach by public transport, while a community centre may offer activities unsuitable for some age groups. Poor maintenance can also discourage visitors. Effective planning therefore requires attention to accessibility, safety, and residents' interests.

C. Some city governments focus on constructing impressive new facilities. Others prefer improving existing spaces that already serve local communities. Both approaches may have value, but investment decisions should consider who benefits and whether the spaces can be maintained. The quality of a community space depends on its usefulness, not simply its appearance.

## Passage C — Managing Tourism in Historic Cities

A. Tourism can support historic cities by creating employment and providing income for local businesses. Visitors may also contribute to the maintenance of cultural attractions through entrance fees. However, large visitor numbers can increase pressure on transport, public spaces, and housing.

B. Residents and businesses may view tourism differently. Hotel owners and restaurant operators often welcome additional customers, while some residents worry about noise and rising accommodation costs. These views are not necessarily incompatible: a city can benefit economically while particular neighbourhoods experience serious difficulties.

C. Local authorities may respond by managing visitor flows, improving public transport, and protecting residential areas. Restricting access to certain locations could reduce pressure, but it might also affect businesses that depend on tourism. Sustainable planning requires balancing economic benefits with the everyday needs of local communities.

- Main Ideas: Xác định thông điệp bao quát của đoạn hoặc toàn bài.
- Matching Headings: Chọn heading đại diện cho toàn bộ đoạn, không chỉ một câu hoặc ví dụ.
- Comparing Information: So sánh lợi ích, hạn chế, quan điểm và giải pháp.
- Problem and Solution: Xác định vấn đề xã hội, nguyên nhân và giải pháp phù hợp.
- Inference: Rút ra kết luận có căn cứ mà không suy luận quá mức.
