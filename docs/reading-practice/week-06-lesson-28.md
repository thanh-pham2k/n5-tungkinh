# Lesson 28 — Job Satisfaction

Focus: Writer's Opinion · Comparing Viewpoints · Main Idea

### Reading Passage

What Makes a Job Worth Keeping?

A. Job satisfaction is often associated with salary, but employees evaluate their work through several different experiences. Income affects financial security and may influence whether someone can remain in a position. However, relationships with colleagues, opportunities to develop skills, and the feeling that work is meaningful can also shape overall satisfaction.

B. Some employees place a high value on pay and promotion. They argue that financial rewards recognize effort and provide practical opportunities outside work. Others consider independence and supportive management more important. A well-paid position may still feel unpleasant if employees have little control over their tasks or regularly experience disrespect.

C. Employers sometimes assume that improving office facilities will make workers happier. Comfortable spaces and useful equipment can certainly remove daily frustrations. Nevertheless, attractive surroundings may have limited influence when employees face unclear expectations or feel that their contributions are ignored. This distinction suggests that visible benefits are not always the same as meaningful improvements in working conditions.

D. Satisfaction may also change during a career. A recent graduate might prioritize training and learning opportunities, while an experienced employee may value stability or flexibility. These patterns are possibilities rather than fixed rules. Two people at similar career stages can still have very different priorities because of personal circumstances.

E. Employers should therefore avoid assuming that one reward will motivate everyone equally. Listening to employees, providing fair treatment, and offering realistic opportunities for development may be more useful than relying on a single benefit. Job satisfaction is not determined by one perfect workplace feature but by how several conditions fit individual needs.

### Paraphrasing

- financial security → economic stability
- recognize effort → acknowledge contributions
- little control over tasks → limited autonomy
- visible benefits → easily observed workplace advantages
- fixed rules → principles assumed to apply universally

### Common Mistakes

- Nhầm opinion với fact: Một nhóm nhân viên coi salary quan trọng không có nghĩa tác giả xem salary quan trọng nhất.
- Khái quát quá mức: Career stage có thể ảnh hưởng priorities, không quyết định tuyệt đối.
- Yes/No/Not Given: Mức tăng lương 10% không xuất hiện trong bài.
