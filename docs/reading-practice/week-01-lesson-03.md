# Lesson 03 — Homework and Students

Focus: True/False/Not Given · Cause and Effect · Supporting Details

### Reading Passage

How Much Homework Is Helpful?

A. Homework has been part of education for generations, yet its purpose continues to be debated. Some teachers believe assignments help students strengthen classroom knowledge, while others worry that excessive work reduces time for rest and personal interests. The central question is not simply whether students should receive homework, but how assignments can contribute meaningfully to learning.

B. Well-designed homework can provide opportunities for independent practice. After learning a mathematical method in class, students may need additional exercises to apply it confidently. Teachers can also use completed assignments to identify misunderstandings. However, repeating large numbers of similar questions may offer limited additional value once a student has understood the concept.

C. Homework affects students differently depending on their circumstances. Some have quiet study spaces and adults who can offer guidance. Others share crowded rooms, care for younger siblings, or lack necessary resources. As a result, equal assignments do not always require equal effort. Teachers who ignore these differences may mistake limited home support for a lack of motivation.

D. Time is another concern. Students who spend many hours completing assignments may have fewer opportunities for physical activity, sleep, or social interaction. Nevertheless, the amount of homework alone does not determine its educational value. A short assignment requiring thoughtful analysis may contribute more to understanding than a lengthy worksheet completed mechanically.

E. Some schools are experimenting with homework policies that emphasize quality rather than quantity. Teachers may coordinate deadlines, provide choices between tasks, or allow certain activities to be completed at school. Such approaches aim to preserve independent practice while reducing unnecessary pressure. Rather than adopting one rule for every student, educators may need to consider age, learning goals, and individual circumstances.

### Paraphrasing

- strengthen classroom knowledge → reinforce what was taught in lessons
- identify misunderstandings → recognize gaps in understanding
- lack necessary resources → have insufficient study materials
- completed mechanically → done without much thought
- quality rather than quantity → effectiveness instead of workload size

### Common Mistakes

- Nhầm False và Not Given: Bài phản đối ý mọi học sinh cần nỗ lực giống nhau, nhưng không cung cấp điểm thi sau cải cách homework.
- Nhầm correlation với cause: Bài tập nhiều không tự động tạo kết quả tốt.
- Bỏ qua từ giới hạn: Some, may, not always khác hoàn toàn all, must, never.
