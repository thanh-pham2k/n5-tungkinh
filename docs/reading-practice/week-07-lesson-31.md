# Lesson 31 — Space Exploration

Focus: Cause and Effect · Matching Features · Evidence vs Hypothesis

### Reading Passage

Why Explore Space?

A. Space exploration began partly because governments wanted to demonstrate technological strength. Today, scientists also use spacecraft to investigate questions about planets, stars, and the origins of the solar system. Supporters argue that exploration expands human knowledge, while critics question whether expensive missions should receive priority over urgent problems on Earth.

B. Different missions serve different purposes. Satellites orbiting Earth collect information about weather patterns and environmental changes. Robotic probes travel to distant destinations and gather measurements without placing astronauts in danger. Human missions, meanwhile, allow astronauts to respond directly to unexpected situations, although they require extensive safety systems and supplies.

C. Scientific discoveries often depend on instruments that can detect things human eyes cannot see. For example, a telescope may identify light from a distant object, but the meaning of that signal requires interpretation. Researchers compare observations with predictions before deciding whether the evidence supports a particular explanation. A surprising measurement can lead to a new hypothesis rather than an immediate conclusion.

D. Exploration also creates technical challenges. Engineers must design equipment that works under extreme temperatures and communicates across enormous distances. Solutions developed for missions may later influence technologies used on Earth. However, not every invention associated with space research becomes widely useful, and benefits can be difficult to predict in advance.

E. The strongest argument for exploration is not that every mission guarantees a practical reward. Instead, space research offers opportunities to test ideas and develop knowledge that would otherwise remain unavailable. Its value should be considered alongside costs, scientific goals, and the quality of evidence produced.

### Paraphrasing

- demonstrate technological strength → show scientific and engineering capability
- gather measurements → collect scientific data
- placing astronauts in danger → exposing human crews to risk
- supports a particular explanation → provides evidence for a proposed interpretation
- practical reward → direct real-world benefit

### Common Mistakes

- Hypothesis vs Conclusion: Một tín hiệu bất thường chưa đủ để đưa ra kết luận cuối cùng.
- Matching Features: Satellite, robotic probe và human mission có nhiệm vụ khác nhau.
- Inference: Không mặc định mọi công nghệ phát triển cho space missions đều hữu ích trên Trái Đất.
