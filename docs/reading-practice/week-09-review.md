## Passage A — A Local Coffee Chain

A. A fictional coffee chain recorded monthly sales from two types of orders. In 2023, drinks purchased inside its shops generated $18,000, while takeaway orders generated $12,000. In 2025, in-shop sales rose to $20,000 and takeaway sales reached $19,000. Total monthly revenue therefore increased from $30,000 to $39,000.

B. The owner noticed that takeaway customers often valued speed, while customers sitting inside the shops paid more attention to comfortable seating and atmosphere. The business introduced a faster collection counter without removing seating areas. This decision reflected the different needs of its customers rather than assuming that one type of service should replace the other.

C. Although takeaway revenue grew rapidly, the owner also monitored packaging and staffing costs. Higher sales could support expansion, but revenue alone did not establish whether the business was becoming more profitable. The company planned to evaluate both expenses and customer satisfaction before opening another branch.

| --- | --- | --- |

| Takeaway | $12,000 | $19,000 |
| Total | $30,000 | $39,000 |

## Passage B — Second-Hand Shopping

A. Buying second-hand products has become attractive to consumers for several reasons. Some want to spend less, while others hope to reduce waste by extending the useful life of products. However, the reasons for choosing second-hand goods may differ between age groups and product categories.

B. In a fictional survey of 300 second-hand shoppers, 50% selected lower prices as their main motivation in 2024, 30% selected environmental concerns, and 20% selected finding unusual products. In 2026, the figures were 45%, 35%, and 20% respectively. Environmental concerns gained importance, but lower prices remained the most frequently selected reason.

C. Customers also considered product condition and seller reliability. A cheaper item was not necessarily attractive if its condition was unclear. Some shoppers preferred detailed photographs and honest descriptions, even when this meant spending more time comparing offers. The findings suggest that affordability matters, but trust also influences purchasing decisions.

| --- | --- | --- |

| Environmental concerns | 30% | 35% |
| Unusual products | 20% | 20% |

## Passage C — Subscription Services

A. Subscription services allow consumers to pay regularly for continued access to products or digital content. Businesses may benefit from predictable income, while customers may enjoy convenient access without making separate purchases each time. However, subscriptions can become expensive when people maintain several services they rarely use.

B. A fictional survey examined how often 400 subscribers reviewed their subscriptions. In 2023, 25% checked their active services every month, 35% checked every few months, and 40% rarely checked. In 2025, monthly checking rose to 40%, checking every few months remained at 35%, and rarely checking declined to 25%.

C. Researchers suggested that growing attention to recurring expenses may have encouraged more frequent reviews. However, the survey did not establish why every respondent changed behaviour. Some participants said they cancelled unused services, while others kept subscriptions because they valued convenience. The results illustrate how consumers may become more attentive to regular spending without abandoning subscription models entirely.

### Data Table

| Review frequency | 2023 | 2025 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every month | 25% | 40% |
| Every few months | 35% | 35% |
| Rarely | 40% | 25% |
