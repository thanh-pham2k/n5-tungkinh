# Lesson 29 — Skills for the Future

Focus: Comparing Viewpoints · Writer's Attitude · Inference

### Reading Passage

Preparing for Jobs That May Change

A. Technological change is influencing the skills people need at work. Some routine tasks can now be completed by software, while new tools create different responsibilities. This has encouraged debate about how schools, universities, and employers should prepare workers for careers that may change repeatedly.

B. One viewpoint emphasizes technical knowledge. Supporters argue that understanding digital tools, data, and specialized systems will help workers participate in technology-driven industries. Without suitable technical skills, employees may struggle to use new equipment or evaluate information produced by automated systems.

C. Another viewpoint gives greater importance to transferable skills such as communication, problem-solving, and adaptability. Supporters of this approach argue that particular technologies may become outdated, while the ability to learn and cooperate remains useful across different roles. However, transferable skills alone may not be sufficient for positions requiring specialized knowledge.

D. Employers face a related decision about training. Some prefer recruiting people who already possess the required skills because immediate productivity matters. Others invest in existing employees, believing that people familiar with the organization can learn new tools effectively. Training requires time and resources, but recruitment can also be costly and may not solve long-term skill shortages.

E. The strongest preparation combines technical competence with the capacity to keep learning. Educational institutions and employers should not treat these skill groups as competitors. Instead, they can provide opportunities to practise new technologies while developing communication and judgment. Future readiness is less about predicting every new job than about helping people respond when requirements change.

### Paraphrasing

- technology-driven industries → sectors strongly influenced by technology
- transferable skills → abilities useful across different jobs
- become outdated → lose relevance over time
- immediate productivity → the ability to contribute without delay
- respond when requirements change → adapt to new expectations

### Common Mistakes

- Nhầm tác giả với supporters: Một nhóm đề cao technical skills không có nghĩa tác giả chỉ ủng hộ nhóm đó.
- Inference: Biết phần mềm hiện tại không bảo đảm thích nghi với phần mềm tương lai.
- False vs Not Given: Bài bác bỏ transferable skills đủ cho mọi nghề, nhưng không đưa dự báo lương năm 2030.
