# Lesson 10 — The Future of Transportation

Focus: Paraphrasing · Sentence Completion · Evaluating Trade-offs

### Reading Passage

Moving Towards Smarter Cities

A. Transportation systems are changing as cities search for ways to reduce pollution and manage increasing travel demand. Electric vehicles, intelligent traffic systems, and automated transport services are often presented as possible solutions. However, improving urban mobility involves more than introducing new technology. City planners must consider infrastructure, cost, and how people actually travel.

B. Electric vehicles produce no exhaust emissions while being driven, which can improve air quality on busy streets. Nevertheless, their overall environmental impact depends partly on how electricity is generated and how batteries are produced. Expanding electric transport also requires charging facilities. Without convenient access to charging, some drivers may hesitate to replace conventional vehicles.

C. Intelligent traffic systems use information from sensors and cameras to adjust traffic signals according to road conditions. By responding to congestion, these systems may reduce unnecessary waiting. Yet they cannot create unlimited road space. If the number of vehicles continues to rise, improved traffic management alone may be insufficient.

D. Public transport remains important because buses and trains can move many passengers using less road space per person than private cars. Some cities are exploring digital applications that combine journey planning, ticket purchasing, and information about different transport services. Such systems may make public transport easier to use, but they are less effective when services are unreliable or routes do not meet people's needs.

E. Future transportation will probably involve a combination of approaches rather than one revolutionary invention. Technology can support cleaner and more convenient journeys, but investment in reliable services and accessible infrastructure is equally important. The central challenge is to design transport networks that work for different communities, not simply to introduce the most advanced vehicles.

### Paraphrasing

- manage increasing travel demand → respond to growing transportation needs
- hesitate to replace conventional vehicles → feel uncertain about switching from traditional cars
- reduce unnecessary waiting → shorten avoidable delays
- routes do not meet people's needs → services fail to serve actual travel patterns
- a combination of approaches → several solutions working together

### Common Mistakes

- Nhầm cause-effect: Số xe tăng có thể khiến giải pháp điều phối giao thông không đủ hiệu quả.
- Nhầm lợi ích cục bộ với tác động tổng thể: EV không thải khí từ ống xả khi chạy, nhưng sản xuất điện và pin vẫn ảnh hưởng môi trường.
- Chọn theo keyword: Có ứng dụng giao thông không đồng nghĩa dịch vụ giao thông đã tốt.
