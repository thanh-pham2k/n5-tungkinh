# Lesson 24 — The Power of Music

Focus: Inference · Multiple Choice · Author's Purpose

### Reading Passage

Why Music Affects Us Differently

A. Music is present in many areas of life, from celebrations and films to exercise and personal relaxation. People often describe music as powerful because it can influence their mood and attention. However, the same piece of music does not necessarily produce identical reactions in everyone. Personal memories, cultural experiences, and the situation in which music is heard can all affect its meaning.

B. Music can become closely connected with memory. A familiar song may remind someone of a particular journey, friendship, or important event. The emotional response may come partly from the remembered experience rather than from the musical features alone. As a result, a song that feels comforting to one listener could make another person feel sad.

C. Some people use music while studying or working. They may believe it helps them concentrate or makes repetitive tasks more enjoyable. Yet the effect can depend on the activity. Music that feels pleasant during a simple task might become distracting when someone is reading difficult material. Individual preferences also matter, so a single recommendation is unlikely to suit every learner.

D. Music is frequently used in shared experiences. Singing together or attending performances can create opportunities for social connection. Participants may feel part of a group even when they have different backgrounds. Nevertheless, this does not mean that everyone enjoys large musical gatherings; some prefer listening privately.

E. The influence of music is therefore best understood through the interaction between sound, personal experience, and context. Music can support enjoyment, memory, and connection, but its effects are not universal. Instead of assuming that one type of music always produces a particular result, people can consider when and why different musical experiences are useful.

### Paraphrasing

- identical reactions → the same responses
- closely connected with memory → strongly associated with recollection
- repetitive tasks → activities performed repeatedly
- feel part of a group → experience social belonging
- effects are not universal → results differ between individuals

### Common Mistakes

- Nhầm evidence với assumption: Một người thư giãn nhờ bài hát không chứng minh mọi người cũng vậy.
- Inference: Bài hát có thể giúp một tác vụ nhưng gây phân tâm ở tác vụ khác.
- Author's Purpose: Ví dụ nghe nhạc riêng và biểu diễn nhóm nhằm nhấn mạnh sự đa dạng trải nghiệm.
