## Passage A — Languages at Risk

A. Languages carry more than everyday information. They can preserve stories, local knowledge, and ways of describing the natural world. When fewer children learn a community's language, some of this knowledge may become harder to pass on. However, a language's future depends on more than the number of people who currently speak it.

B. In one fictional region, a local language was spoken regularly by 60% of residents over 60, but by only 20% of residents aged 15–25. Community groups responded by organising conversation clubs and recording stories from older speakers. Schools also introduced optional lessons, although participation varied between districts.

C. Some supporters argued that digital recordings could preserve valuable material for future generations. Others emphasised that a language survives most effectively when people use it in ordinary conversations. Both approaches may contribute, but recordings alone cannot guarantee that younger residents will become confident speakers. The author suggests combining documentation with opportunities for active use.

## Passage B — The Development of Public Libraries

A. Libraries have long provided access to written information, but their functions have changed with society. In some earlier periods, collections were available mainly to scholars or wealthy individuals. As public education expanded, libraries increasingly served wider communities. Their value became connected not only to storing books but also to making knowledge accessible.

B. Modern libraries may offer digital resources, study spaces, language classes, and community events. These services can support people who lack quiet places to work or reliable internet access at home. However, adding new services creates demands on staff and budgets. Libraries must decide which activities best serve their communities.

C. Some critics believe libraries should concentrate on books and reading. Others see them as flexible public spaces that can respond to changing needs. The author argues that these goals are not necessarily opposed. Reading collections remain important, but services that improve access to learning may strengthen rather than weaken the library's original purpose.

## Passage C — Restoring an Ancient Market

A. A fictional city planned to restore an old market that had operated for more than a century. The building was valued for its distinctive roof and its role in local trade. However, damaged walls and outdated facilities made it difficult for traders to continue working safely.

B. The council considered two proposals. Plan A would preserve most original features and improve safety, costing $2 million. Plan B would make larger changes to create additional commercial space, costing $3 million. Supporters of Plan B expected higher rental income, while supporters of Plan A argued that the market's historical character was its greatest attraction.

C. After public consultation, the council selected Plan A but added accessible entrances and improved lighting. Some traders remained concerned about disruption during construction. The decision illustrated a compromise: preserving historical identity while making the market safer and more practical. The author suggests that restoration should be evaluated by long-term community value, not construction cost alone.

### Kỹ năng tổng hợp từ Week 1–9

- Main Idea & Matching Headings: Xác định thông điệp bao quát, không chọn một chi tiết nhỏ.

- True/False/Not Given & Yes/No/Not Given: Phân biệt thông tin được xác nhận, bị bác bỏ và không được đề cập.

- Problem and Solution: Đánh giá giải pháp dựa trên vấn đề và các ràng buộc được nêu.
