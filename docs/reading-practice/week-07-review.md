## Passage A — Studying Ancient Climates

A. Scientists investigate ancient climates because direct temperature records cover only a limited part of Earth's history. To study earlier periods, researchers examine natural materials that preserve clues about past conditions. These clues are known as proxy evidence because they provide indirect information rather than direct measurements of ancient temperatures.

B. Different sources reveal different features. Ice cores contain layers that can preserve information about past atmospheric conditions. Tree rings may show changes associated with growing conditions, although ring width can be influenced by several factors. Sediment layers can preserve materials that help researchers investigate environmental changes over long periods.

C. No single source gives a complete history. Scientists compare evidence from different locations and materials to examine whether a proposed climate pattern is consistent. If one tree-ring record suggests a dry period, researchers may look for supporting information elsewhere before reaching a broad conclusion. This approach reduces the risk of treating a local observation as a global explanation.

## Passage B — Discovering New Medicines

A. The development of a new medicine may begin when researchers identify a substance that appears to influence a biological process. Laboratory investigations can help scientists understand its possible effects. However, an encouraging result in a controlled laboratory environment does not automatically establish that the substance will work safely in people.

B. Researchers use different stages of investigation to answer different questions. Early laboratory work explores possible mechanisms. Further studies examine safety and effectiveness under more demanding conditions. A substance may perform well in one stage but produce disappointing results later, particularly when the situation becomes more complex.

C. Scientific progress therefore involves rejecting ideas as well as confirming them. An unsuccessful trial can reveal limitations in an original hypothesis or show that a method needs improvement. Although this may delay the development process, identifying problems before wider use is an important part of responsible research.

## Passage C — How Scientists Study Animal Migration

A. Animal migration can involve movement across large distances, making it difficult to study through direct observation alone. Researchers may use tracking devices to record where animals travel. They can also examine environmental information, such as seasonal conditions, to investigate possible influences on migration routes.

B. Different methods answer different questions. Tracking devices provide information about movement patterns, while environmental records help researchers compare those patterns with changing conditions. Direct field observations can provide detailed information about behaviour at particular locations. However, no method necessarily explains why an animal chooses a specific route.

C. Suppose researchers observe that a group of birds changes its route during an unusually dry season. This relationship may suggest that environmental conditions influence movement, but other factors could also be involved. Scientists may need observations across several seasons before drawing stronger conclusions. The goal is to build explanations that reflect the available evidence rather than assume that one pattern reveals a complete cause.

### Kỹ năng cần đạt

- Hypothesis: Giả thuyết cần được kiểm tra, chưa phải sự thật.
- Evidence: Dữ liệu hoặc quan sát hỗ trợ một giải thích.
- Conclusion: Kết luận phải phù hợp với mức độ bằng chứng hiện có.
- Cause and Effect: Phân biệt quan hệ nhân quả với sự tương quan.
- Matching Features: Ghép đúng công cụ, nhà nghiên cứu hoặc phương pháp với chức năng thực tế.
- Inference: Suy luận hợp lý nhưng không vượt quá nội dung passage.

Mục tiêu luyện tập: 39–44/55 câu (70–80%). Với câu hỏi khoa học, hãy luôn tự hỏi: “Is this a hypothesis, an observation, or a conclusion supported by evidence?”
