# Lesson 12 — Climate Change

Focus: Cause and Effect · TFNG · Problem and Solution

### Reading Passage

Responding to a Changing Climate

A. Climate change refers to long-term changes in temperature and weather patterns. Although natural factors can influence the climate, human activities have become a major cause of recent global warming. Burning fossil fuels releases greenhouse gases that trap additional heat in the atmosphere. The resulting changes affect natural systems and human communities, but their consequences are not identical everywhere.

B. Rising temperatures can increase the likelihood or intensity of certain extreme weather events. Some regions face longer periods of heat, while others experience changes in rainfall patterns. Farmers may struggle when familiar growing seasons become less predictable. However, an individual storm or unusually cold day cannot, by itself, establish a long-term climate trend.

C. Coastal communities face particular challenges as sea levels rise. Higher water levels can increase flooding risks, especially when combined with storms. Some cities are constructing protective barriers or restoring coastal wetlands to reduce damage. These measures can help communities adapt, but they may be expensive and cannot eliminate every risk.

D. Addressing climate change involves two related approaches. Mitigation aims to limit future warming by reducing greenhouse gas emissions. Adaptation focuses on preparing for changes that are already occurring or expected. For example, replacing fossil-fuel electricity with cleaner energy supports mitigation, while developing heat-resistant crops helps farmers adapt to warmer conditions.

E. Climate policies also involve difficult decisions about resources and fairness. Wealthier communities may have greater capacity to invest in protection, while poorer populations can face serious risks despite contributing relatively little to emissions. Effective responses therefore require cooperation, long-term planning, and attention to the different needs of affected communities.

### Paraphrasing

- trap additional heat → retain more warmth
- less predictable → increasingly uncertain
- reduce flooding risks → lower the likelihood of flood damage
- limit future warming → prevent further temperature increases
- greater capacity to invest → more financial ability to fund protection

### Common Mistakes

- Weather vs Climate: Một ngày lạnh không phủ định xu hướng nóng lên dài hạn.
- Mitigation vs Adaptation: Giảm emissions là mitigation; chuẩn bị đối phó hậu quả là adaptation.
- False vs Not Given: Không thể loại bỏ toàn bộ rủi ro là False đối với câu khẳng định bảo vệ hoàn toàn; thời gian hoàn thành công trình là Not Given.
