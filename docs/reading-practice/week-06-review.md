## Passage A — Should Companies Monitor Employees?

A. Digital monitoring tools allow employers to collect information about working hours, computer activity, and task completion. Supporters argue that these systems can help managers identify delays and distribute workloads more fairly. They also suggest that monitoring may provide useful evidence when employees work remotely and supervisors cannot observe daily activities directly.

B. Critics question whether such measurements represent meaningful productivity. An employee who spends considerable time solving a difficult problem may appear less active than someone completing many simple tasks. Monitoring can also create pressure if workers feel that every moment must be recorded. Some employees may begin focusing on visible activity rather than valuable results.

C. The most reasonable approach is to distinguish useful performance information from unnecessary surveillance. Employers should explain what is measured, why it matters, and how the information will be used. Monitoring may have a legitimate role in some workplaces, but collecting more data does not automatically lead to better management. Trust and clear expectations remain important.

## Passage B — Is Changing Careers a Risk or an Opportunity?

A. Career changes are sometimes viewed as signs of uncertainty, especially when employees leave established positions. Supporters of career mobility argue that moving into a new field can provide opportunities for learning and personal development. They also suggest that experience from one industry may help people approach problems differently in another.

B. Others emphasize the difficulties of changing direction. A person may need additional training, accept a lower initial salary, or spend time building professional relationships. These costs can be especially important for individuals with financial responsibilities. However, remaining in a familiar occupation may also involve risks if the work becomes less available or no longer matches personal goals.

C. Career changes should therefore be evaluated according to individual circumstances rather than treated as either brave or irresponsible. People can compare the skills they already possess with those required in a new field and consider whether they have enough resources for a transition. A carefully planned change may be reasonable, but the decision does not have to be appropriate for everyone.

## Passage C — The Value of Workplace Feedback

A. Feedback is often considered an important part of professional development. Some managers prefer direct criticism because they believe employees need clear information about mistakes. Others emphasize encouragement, arguing that workers may become less willing to experiment when they fear negative reactions. Both approaches aim to improve performance, but they focus on different concerns.

B. The usefulness of feedback depends partly on its content and delivery. A comment such as “Your report needs improvement” identifies a problem but provides little guidance. Explaining which section lacks evidence and suggesting a way to strengthen it gives the employee a clearer direction. Timing also matters: feedback delivered long after a task may be harder to connect with the original decisions.

C. Effective feedback does not require avoiding criticism or offering praise at every opportunity. Instead, it should be specific, respectful, and connected to achievable improvements. Employees also need opportunities to ask questions and discuss expectations. Feedback works best when it supports learning rather than simply judging past performance.

### Kỹ năng cần đạt

- Writer's Opinion: Phân biệt lập trường của tác giả với ý kiến của supporters, critics, managers hoặc employees.
- Yes/No/Not Given: Yes = tác giả đồng ý; No = tác giả thể hiện quan điểm trái ngược; Not Given = không đủ thông tin xác định.
- Comparing Viewpoints: Nhận diện sự khác biệt về ưu tiên, lý do và lập luận giữa các nhóm.
- Writer's Attitude: Phân biệt cautiously supportive, critical, neutral và strongly opposed.
- Inference: Suy luận dựa trên lập luận thực sự được nêu, không gán thêm quan điểm cho tác giả.

Mục tiêu luyện tập: 39–44/55 câu (70–80%). Với mỗi câu hỏi về quan điểm, hãy xác định trước: “Who believes this — the writer, a supporter, or a critic?”
