# Lesson 47 — The History of Writing

Focus: Matching Information · Sentence Completion · Scientific/Historical Reasoning

### Reading Passage

From Marks to Messages

A. Writing transformed the ways societies stored and shared information. Before written records became common, people relied heavily on memory and spoken communication. Oral traditions could preserve important stories, but detailed information sometimes changed as it passed between generations. Writing provided another method of recording events, agreements, and knowledge.

B. Early writing systems developed gradually in different societies. Some began with marks representing goods or quantities, particularly for trade and administration. Over time, certain systems became capable of expressing more complex ideas. This development did not happen everywhere in the same way or at the same speed.

C. The materials used for writing influenced how information was recorded. Clay tablets could preserve records for long periods but were not always convenient to transport. Other materials, including plant-based surfaces, were lighter and easier to carry, although their survival depended on storage conditions. The choice of material therefore involved trade-offs between durability and practicality.

D. Writing was initially available to a limited number of people in many societies. Training could be time-consuming, and specialised writers often worked for institutions or powerful individuals. As educational opportunities and writing technologies developed, written communication became accessible to larger populations in some regions. However, access remained unequal.

E. Writing did not replace spoken language. Instead, it expanded the ways information could be preserved, examined, and transmitted. Historians study surviving records to understand earlier societies, but they must consider who produced those records and whose experiences were excluded. Written evidence is valuable, yet it never represents every perspective automatically.

### Paraphrasing

- stored and shared information → preserved and communicated knowledge
- developed gradually → emerged over time
- convenient to transport → easy to carry
- accessible to larger populations → available to more people
- experiences were excluded → viewpoints were left unrepresented

### Common Mistakes

- Matching Information: Tìm ý tương đương, không chỉ từ giống nhau.
- Historical evidence: Văn bản còn tồn tại không phản ánh đầy đủ mọi nhóm người.
- Inference: Không suy ra mọi hệ thống chữ viết đều có cùng nguồn gốc.
