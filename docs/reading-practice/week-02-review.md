## Passage A — Smart Homes and Everyday Decisions

A. Smart home devices allow people to control lighting, heating, and household equipment through connected systems. Some devices learn from repeated behaviour and adjust settings automatically. For example, a heating system may reduce energy use when residents are usually away. This can improve convenience and potentially lower household expenses.

B. However, automatic decisions are not always appropriate. If a family's daily routine changes, a system relying on previous patterns may adjust the temperature at an inconvenient time. Residents may need to update settings or temporarily take manual control. The effectiveness of smart technology therefore depends partly on how accurately it reflects current household behaviour.

C. Connected devices also raise security concerns. Information about when residents are home or away may be stored by service providers. If these records are not protected, they could reveal sensitive details about personal routines. Some households therefore prefer systems that allow greater control over data collection. Smart homes can offer useful assistance, but convenience should not remove users' ability to understand and manage automated functions.

## Passage B — Digital Payments and Small Businesses

A. Digital payments have changed how customers purchase everyday goods. Instead of carrying cash, shoppers can use cards or mobile applications. For small businesses, electronic transactions may reduce the need to handle physical money and make it easier to review sales records. Customers may also appreciate faster payment during busy periods.

B. Nevertheless, accepting digital payments involves costs. Some providers charge businesses transaction fees, while suitable equipment and reliable internet connections may be necessary. These requirements can create difficulties for shops operating with limited financial resources. In addition, technical failures may temporarily prevent customers from completing purchases.

C. Digital payment records can help shop owners understand purchasing patterns. However, these records do not automatically explain why customers choose particular products. A rise in sales might reflect seasonal demand, a promotion, or another factor. Business owners therefore need to interpret transaction information alongside other evidence rather than assuming that numerical patterns reveal every cause.

## Passage C — Drones in Environmental Research

A. Researchers increasingly use drones to observe forests, rivers, and coastal areas. Equipped with cameras and sensors, drones can collect information from places that are difficult to reach on foot. In some situations, they also reduce the need for researchers to enter dangerous environments. This makes them useful tools for gathering environmental information.

B. Despite these advantages, drones have limitations. Strong winds and heavy rain can interrupt flights, while battery capacity restricts how long many devices can operate. Images collected from above may also fail to reveal conditions beneath dense vegetation. As a result, drone observations sometimes need to be combined with measurements taken directly on the ground.

C. Another challenge is interpreting the collected information. A photograph showing changes in forest colour does not necessarily explain what caused them. Researchers may need additional observations to distinguish between seasonal variation, disease, and environmental damage. Drones can expand the amount of available evidence, but they do not remove the need for scientific reasoning.
