# Lesson 25 — How Emotions Affect Decisions

Focus: Inference · Cause and Effect · Author's Conclusion

### Reading Passage

Decisions Are Not Purely Logical

A. People often believe that good decisions result from carefully comparing facts and choosing the most reasonable option. Information and logical thinking are important, but emotions also influence how people interpret situations. Feelings can draw attention to particular risks or opportunities. Their influence is not always harmful, although strong emotions may sometimes lead people to overlook relevant information.

B. Fear can affect decisions by making possible losses seem especially important. Someone considering a new job might focus heavily on the uncertainty of changing workplaces while paying less attention to potential benefits. This caution may protect the person from unnecessary risk, but it could also discourage a worthwhile opportunity. The result depends partly on whether the perceived danger matches the available evidence.

C. Excitement can influence decisions in a different direction. A person attracted by a special offer may concentrate on the possibility of saving money while ignoring whether the product is actually needed. The emotional appeal of the opportunity can make immediate benefits feel more important than longer-term consequences. However, enthusiasm can also motivate people to explore possibilities they might otherwise avoid.

D. Emotional awareness may help people make more balanced choices. Recognizing feelings does not require ignoring them. Instead, individuals can consider why they feel strongly about an option and whether their interpretation is supported by facts. Taking time before an important decision may also provide an opportunity to examine alternatives more carefully.

E. The relationship between emotion and reasoning is therefore more complicated than a simple conflict between feelings and facts. Emotions can provide useful signals, but they should not be treated as complete evidence. Better decisions may result from understanding emotional reactions while checking assumptions against available information.

### Paraphrasing

- possible losses seem especially important → potential disadvantages receive greater attention
- perceived danger → risk as understood by the individual
- longer-term consequences → outcomes that appear later
- examine alternatives → consider other available options
- checking assumptions against information → testing beliefs using evidence

### Common Mistakes

- Nhầm emotion với evidence: Cảm thấy nguy hiểm không chứng minh tình huống thực sự nguy hiểm.
- Inference quá mức: Một quyết định mua hàng thiếu cân nhắc không chứng minh người đó luôn thiếu lý trí.
- Author's Conclusion: Bài không yêu cầu loại bỏ cảm xúc mà yêu cầu đánh giá chúng cùng dữ kiện.
