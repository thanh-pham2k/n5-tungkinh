# Lesson 35 — Scientific Discoveries

Focus: Scientific Reasoning · Comparing Methods · Author's Conclusion

### Reading Passage

How Discoveries Become Scientific Knowledge

A. Scientific discoveries are sometimes presented as sudden moments when a researcher finds an unexpected answer. In practice, discoveries often develop through repeated questions, observations, and tests. A surprising result may begin an investigation, but it does not automatically become accepted scientific knowledge.

B. Scientists use different approaches depending on the question. Controlled experiments attempt to examine the effect of one factor while keeping other conditions as consistent as possible. Observational studies, by contrast, investigate patterns that already occur without necessarily changing the situation. Both approaches can provide valuable information, although they support different kinds of conclusions.

C. For example, researchers might observe that people who spend more time outdoors report better concentration. This pattern suggests a possible relationship, but it does not establish that outdoor time directly causes improved concentration. Other factors may influence both behaviours. A carefully designed experiment could help examine the proposed causal explanation more directly.

D. Results also need to be checked. A single study may produce an unusual finding because of measurement problems, limited samples, or circumstances that do not apply elsewhere. When independent researchers investigate a similar question and obtain comparable results, confidence in the explanation may increase. Even then, conclusions can be revised when stronger evidence becomes available.

E. The strength of science lies not in avoiding uncertainty but in managing it carefully. Researchers develop hypotheses, test explanations, and revise conclusions when necessary. Scientific knowledge becomes more dependable through transparent methods and repeated examination, not simply because an idea sounds convincing or comes from a respected expert.

### Paraphrasing

- sudden moments → unexpected breakthroughs
- controlled experiments → tests that manage other conditions
- patterns that already occur → naturally observed relationships
- directly causes → produces an effect
- comparable results → similar findings
- revise conclusions → update interpretations

### Common Mistakes

- Correlation ≠ Causation: Hai hiện tượng cùng xuất hiện không chứng minh một hiện tượng gây ra hiện tượng kia.
- Matching Features: Observational study ghi nhận patterns; controlled experiment kiểm tra tác động có kiểm soát.
- Scientific Reasoning: Nhiều nghiên cứu đồng thuận làm tăng độ tin cậy nhưng không khiến kết luận bất biến.
