# Lesson 43 — The Sharing Economy

Focus: Data and Details · Comparing Trends · Benefits and Limitations

### Reading Passage

Using Resources Without Owning Them

A. The sharing economy describes arrangements in which people access goods or services through temporary use rather than permanent ownership. Examples include renting bicycles, sharing workspaces, and booking accommodation for short stays. Supporters argue that these systems can make underused resources more productive. Critics question whether convenience always produces fair outcomes for users and providers.

B. A fictional city survey compared three shared services in 2022 and 2025. Shared-bicycle use increased from 20,000 to 32,000 monthly trips. Shared-workspace bookings rose from 8,000 to 11,000 per month. Short-stay accommodation bookings increased from 15,000 to 18,000. Bicycle sharing therefore recorded the largest absolute increase, although all three services grew.

C. Users reported different motivations. In 2025, 50% of bicycle-sharing customers selected convenience as their main reason, while 30% chose lower costs and 20% chose environmental concerns. For shared workspaces, flexible access was the most common reason. These findings suggest that the same economic model can attract users for different purposes.

D. Growth can also create problems. Popular shared bicycles may be unavailable during busy periods, while poorly parked bicycles can block pavements. Short-stay accommodation may provide additional income for property owners, but residents sometimes worry about noise or reduced availability of long-term housing. The effects depend on how services are managed and regulated.

E. The sharing economy should not be judged by growth figures alone. A successful system must provide reliable access while considering impacts on workers, residents, and public spaces. Cities and businesses may need rules that balance flexibility with responsibility.

### Paraphrasing

- permanent ownership → owning something long-term
- monthly usage → number of uses each month
- largest absolute increase → greatest rise in actual numbers
- underused resources → assets that are not fully utilised
- balance flexibility with responsibility → combine convenience with appropriate management

### Common Mistakes

- Absolute growth vs Percentage growth: Mức tăng số lượt khác với tỷ lệ tăng.
- Đơn vị: Trips và bookings không nhất thiết là số khách hàng riêng biệt.
- Inference: Dịch vụ tăng trưởng không chứng minh mọi tác động xã hội đều tích cực.
