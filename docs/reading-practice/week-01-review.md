## Passage A — A Library That Became a Learning Centre

A. When a public library in the fictional town of Bellford noticed fewer visitors borrowing printed books, its managers considered reducing opening hours. Instead, they asked residents what services they needed. Many teenagers wanted quiet places to study, while older residents requested help with everyday digital tasks. The library therefore redesigned part of its building to include shared worktables, computer stations, and small rooms for group discussions.

B. The changes attracted different groups of users. Students began meeting after school to prepare presentations, and volunteers organized workshops on using online services. Printed books remained available, although some shelves were moved to create more space. Library staff noticed that visitors increasingly used several services during one visit. A person might borrow a novel, attend a workshop, and then use a computer.

C. Not everyone welcomed the redesign. Some long-term visitors complained that group activities made certain areas noisier. In response, the library introduced separate quiet zones and scheduled workshops at particular times. The managers concluded that the library's role had expanded rather than disappeared. Its challenge was to serve changing community needs without neglecting people who still valued traditional reading spaces.

## Passage B — Should Students Choose Their Own Subjects?

A. Many secondary schools require students to study a broad range of subjects. Supporters of this approach argue that young people may discover interests they would otherwise overlook. A student who initially dislikes science, for example, might become interested after completing an environmental project. Studying several disciplines can also help learners understand connections between different areas of knowledge.

B. Other educators believe students should have greater freedom to choose subjects as they grow older. They argue that personal choice can increase motivation and allow students to explore particular strengths. However, choosing too early may limit future opportunities. A teenager who avoids mathematics might later discover an interest in a career that requires it.

C. Some schools therefore offer a combination of compulsory subjects and optional courses. This model aims to provide a common educational foundation while respecting individual interests. Its effectiveness depends partly on the range of options available. Schools with limited teaching staff may struggle to offer the same choices as larger institutions. The debate is therefore about both student freedom and the resources needed to make that freedom meaningful.

## Passage C — Learning Through Community Projects

A. A group of students joined a project to improve a neglected public garden near their school. Before beginning, they interviewed local residents and examined how the space was being used. Some residents wanted more seating, while others preferred an area for growing vegetables. The students had to consider these different opinions before preparing their proposal.

B. The project connected several school subjects. Students used mathematics to estimate costs, science to investigate suitable plants, and language skills to present their recommendations. Their first plan exceeded the available budget, so they revised it by selecting less expensive materials. Teachers assessed both the final proposal and the reasoning behind the decisions.

C. Although the project created enthusiasm, it also presented difficulties. A few students initially dominated discussions, leaving quieter classmates with limited opportunities to contribute. Teachers introduced rotating responsibilities to improve participation. The experience demonstrated that practical projects can support academic learning and cooperation, but these benefits require careful organization rather than simply placing students in groups.

## Tổng kết Week 01

Kỹ năng trọng tâm: Skimming để nhận diện main idea, Scanning để xác định supporting details, phân biệt thông tin bị phủ định trực tiếp (False) với thông tin không được cung cấp (Not Given), và suy luận dựa trên evidence.

Cách luyện đề xuất: Làm mỗi lesson trong 15–20 phút. Khi kiểm tra đáp án, hãy xác định chính xác đoạn chứa evidence và tự giải thích vì sao ba lựa chọn còn lại không phù hợp.
