## Passage A — Restoring a Damaged River

A. A river flowing through the fictional town of Greenford had become increasingly polluted after years of urban growth. Wastewater from nearby buildings and rubbish carried by rain entered the water. Residents complained about unpleasant smells, while local environmental groups noticed that some aquatic species were becoming less common. Town officials initially considered cleaning the riverbanks more frequently, but researchers argued that removing visible rubbish would not address every source of pollution.

B. The town introduced a broader restoration programme. It improved wastewater treatment, installed rubbish traps in drainage channels, and planted vegetation along sections of the river. The plants helped reduce soil erosion and slowed some rainwater runoff. Schools also organized activities to explain how household waste could affect local waterways.

C. After several years, the river appeared cleaner, and residents reported using nearby walking paths more often. However, researchers warned that appearance alone could not establish whether the ecosystem had recovered. They continued measuring water quality and monitoring aquatic organisms. The project demonstrated that environmental restoration requires both prevention and long-term assessment.

## Passage B — The Challenge of Food Waste

A. Food waste occurs at different stages between production and consumption. Some crops are damaged during transportation, while supermarkets may reject products that do not meet appearance standards. At home, consumers sometimes purchase more food than they can use. These different causes mean that one solution is unlikely to work equally well everywhere.

B. Businesses can reduce waste by improving storage conditions and planning purchases more carefully. Some retailers also offer discounted products approaching their recommended selling dates. Households may benefit from preparing shopping lists and understanding food labels. However, reducing waste does not mean ignoring food safety; products must still be stored and handled appropriately.

C. Food waste also has environmental consequences. Resources such as water, land, and energy are used to produce food that may never be eaten. When discarded food decomposes under certain conditions, it can release greenhouse gases. Preventing unnecessary waste can therefore reduce pressure on resources as well as disposal systems.

## Passage C — Tourism and Fragile Natural Areas

A. Nature tourism can provide income for communities living near forests, mountains, and coastal environments. Visitors may pay for accommodation, local guides, and transport services. This income can create incentives to protect natural attractions. Nevertheless, a growing number of tourists may also place pressure on the environments they come to enjoy.

B. Popular walking routes can suffer from soil erosion when large numbers of visitors repeatedly use the same paths. Wildlife may also be disturbed by noise or inappropriate behaviour. Some destinations respond by limiting visitor numbers, building marked trails, and providing information about responsible conduct. These measures aim to reduce damage while maintaining opportunities for tourism.

C. Restrictions can create difficulties for local businesses that depend on visitors. For this reason, conservation authorities may consult residents before introducing new rules. The most effective approach may involve balancing environmental protection with economic needs rather than maximizing visitor numbers. Tourism can support conservation, but only when its activities remain compatible with the condition of the natural area.

### Kỹ năng cần đạt

- Matching Information: Xác định đoạn chứa ý tương đương, không chỉ tìm từ khóa giống nhau.
- True/False/Not Given: Phân biệt thông tin được xác nhận, bị phủ định và không có đủ bằng chứng.
- Problem and Solution: Xác định vấn đề, nguyên nhân, biện pháp xử lý và giới hạn của giải pháp.
- Inference: Suy luận về tác động môi trường dựa trên dữ kiện, không dùng giả định ngoài passage.

Mục tiêu luyện tập: Đạt khoảng 39–44/55 câu (70–80%). Với mỗi câu sai, hãy tìm lại evidence và xác định lỗi thuộc paraphrasing, thiếu thông tin, nhầm nguyên nhân–kết quả hay suy luận quá mức.
