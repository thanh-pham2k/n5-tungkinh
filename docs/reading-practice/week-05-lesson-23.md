# Lesson 23 — Why People Form Habits

Focus: Cause and Effect · Inference · Evidence vs Assumption

### Reading Passage

How Repeated Actions Become Automatic

A. Much of everyday behaviour consists of actions repeated in familiar situations. People may check their phones after waking up, prepare a drink before work, or take the same route home. Over time, some repeated actions require less conscious attention. These habits can make daily life more efficient, although they may also preserve behaviours that people would prefer to change.

B. Habits often develop through connections between situations and actions. A particular time, place, or feeling may become a signal that encourages a familiar response. For example, someone who regularly buys a snack while waiting for a bus may begin to associate the bus stop with eating. Eventually, the person might feel an urge to buy something even without being particularly hungry.

C. Repetition matters, but frequency alone does not explain every habit. The environment can make certain behaviours easier than others. A person who keeps a water bottle on a desk may drink water more often simply because it is immediately available. Similarly, keeping distracting applications out of sight may reduce the temptation to open them during study sessions.

D. Changing a habit can be difficult because the original situation continues to trigger familiar behaviour. Instead of relying entirely on willpower, people may benefit from adjusting the environment or replacing an unwanted action with a more useful one. For example, someone who automatically checks messages during breaks might choose to take a short walk instead. Such changes require practice and may not work immediately.

E. Habit formation is therefore neither entirely automatic nor completely controlled by conscious decisions. Repeated actions, environmental signals, and practical opportunities all contribute. Understanding these influences can help people design routines that support their goals rather than depending only on motivation.

### Paraphrasing

- less conscious attention → reduced deliberate effort
- familiar response → usual reaction
- immediately available → easy to access
- out of sight → not directly visible
- relying entirely on willpower → depending only on self-control

### Common Mistakes

- Nhầm trigger với outcome: Bus stop là tín hiệu gợi hành vi, không phải bằng chứng người đó đang đói.
- Suy luận quá mức: Giảm kiểm tra điện thoại không chứng minh ý chí tăng trong mọi lĩnh vực.
- Nhầm evidence và assumption: Không có con số ngày cụ thể để hình thành thói quen.
